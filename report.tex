\begin{abstract}
Tox21 is a benchmark for predicting twelve in-vitro toxicity endpoints directly from molecular structure. The task is characterized by extensive label missingness (17.1\% missing), severe class imbalance (positive rates ranging from 2.9\% to 16.2\%), and structural scaffold clustering. In this work, we present \textbf{Toxion}, an end-to-end framework integrating graph featurization, Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNN) with edge-matrix caching, multimodal fusion with Morgan circular fingerprints and physicochemical descriptors, self-supervised pretraining, and post-hoc temperature calibration. Evaluated on a leak-free Bemis--Murcko scaffold split across 7{,}823 curated molecules, our Multimodal Fusion model achieves a mean validation ROC-AUC of 0.8153 (PR-AUC 0.4616) and a held-out test ROC-AUC of 0.7753 (PR-AUC 0.3860), outperforming a rigorous 5-fold cross-validated logistic regression baseline (0.7616 $\pm$ 0.0282). Significant improvements are demonstrated on difficult nuclear receptor targets such as NR-ER (+10.5\% ROC-AUC gain) and stress-response pathways such as SR-p53 (+5.3\% gain). Post-hoc temperature scaling reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) to 0.0276 (val) and 0.0332 (test). Furthermore, self-supervised masked-atom pretraining on 30{,}000 ZINC250k molecules achieves 89.91\% element recovery accuracy, providing effective structural inductive biases for toxicity screening.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Computational toxicology aims to prioritize chemical compounds for empirical bioassays and identify environmental toxins and pharmacological liabilities prior to animal or clinical testing. The Toxicology in the 21st Century (Tox21) data challenge~\cite{huang2016tox21} established a standard public benchmark comprising twelve in-vitro assays targeting nuclear receptors (NR) and stress response (SR) pathways across approximately 10{,}000 compounds.

Predictive modeling on Tox21 presents three fundamental challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Extreme Sparsity and Missingness:} Label annotations are incomplete (17.1\% of all molecule--assay entries are missing). Untested pairs must be rigorously treated as unobserved rather than inactive to prevent structural estimation bias.
    \item \textbf{Severe Class Imbalance:} Active hits constitute as few as 2.9\% of screened compounds (e.g., NR-PPAR-$\gamma$). Under such class skew, classification accuracy is deceptive, necessitating threshold-free ranking metrics such as ROC-AUC and Precision-Recall AUC (PR-AUC).
    \item \textbf{Scaffold Clustering and Generalization:} Chemical datasets exhibit high structural clustering. Traditional random splits leak chemical analogues between training and test sets, artificially inflating benchmark performance. Scaffold-based partitioning is required to measure genuine out-of-distribution generalization~\cite{yang2019analyzing}.
\end{enumerate}

To address these challenges, we develop \textbf{Toxion}, an extensible molecular toxicity platform. We formulate a Multimodal Fusion network combining: (i) a Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN) operating on atom--bond topological graphs with cached edge-transformation matrices and GRU updates, (ii) 2048-bit Morgan circular fingerprints, (iii) RDKit physicochemical descriptors, and (iv) structural toxicophore alerts. We benchmark Toxion against a 5-fold cross-validated linear fingerprint baseline, validate model reliability via temperature scaling and Monte Carlo dropout, and evaluate self-supervised masked-atom pretraining on external chemical libraries.

\subsection{Chemical Diversity and Scaffold Distribution}
We inspect the chemical manifold using 2048-bit Morgan circular fingerprints (radius 2) and pairwise Tanimoto similarity:
\begin{equation}
T(a,b) = \frac{|a \cap b|}{|a| + |b| - |a \cap b|}.
\end{equation}
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) via Singular Value Decomposition on centered fingerprint vectors reveals that the first three components explain only 4.15\%, 3.30\%, and 2.75\% of variance, with 815 components required to capture 90\% of variance. The mean nearest-neighbor similarity across the library is 0.6069 (median 0.6000), with 28.21\% of compounds possessing a close structural neighbor ($T > 0.70$). Mean internal diversity ($1 - \bar{T}$) is 0.9188.

Bemis--Murcko scaffold decomposition~\cite{bemis1996murcko} identifies 2{,}325 unique scaffolds across 7{,}823 compounds. However, the distribution is highly skewed: 1{,}775 compounds (22.69\%) are acyclic (empty scaffold), and 1{,}772 scaffolds occur only once. The Shannon entropy over scaffold frequencies is $H = 7.354$ bits, yielding an effective scaffold count of $2^H = 163.64$.

\subsection{Leak-Free Scaffold Partitioning}
Standard random splitting algorithms create catastrophic data leakage by allocating near-isomers to both training and test partitions. Conversely, standard greedy scaffold clustering places large clusters in held-out sets, frequently leaving validation splits depleted.

Toxion implements a balanced allocation rule: scaffold clusters exceeding 50\% of the validation target size are deterministically assigned to the training partition, while remaining scaffold groups are shuffled. Evaluated on the curated 7{,}823 compounds, this strategy reliably produces 6{,}258 training, 782 validation, and 783 test compounds with strictly zero scaffold overlap between splits.

\subsection{Graph Representation and Atom/Bond Featurization}
Each chemical structure is mapped to a directed graph $G = (V, E)$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Atom Attributes ($d_v = 21$):} One-hot encodings of atomic species (C, N, O, S, F, Cl, Br, I, P, other), degree ($0 \dots 5$), formal charge, implicit valence, hybridization ($sp, sp^2, sp^3$), aromaticity, and number of bonded hydrogens.
    \item \textbf{Bond Attributes ($d_e = 5$):} One-hot encodings of bond order (single, double, triple, aromatic) and ring membership. Every covalent bond is represented as two opposite directed edges.
    \item \textbf{Extended v2 Descriptors ($d_v=50, d_e=10$):} Incorporates periodic table properties (covalent radius, atomic mass, outer electrons, electronegativity), stereocenter chiral tags, and conjugated $\pi$-systems.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN)}
We employ a Message Passing Neural Network with precomputed edge transformations. For each edge $(v, w) \in E$, an MLP maps bond features $e_{vw}$ to an edge transformation matrix $A(e_{vw}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$. Because bond attributes remain invariant during forward passes, $A(e_{vw})$ is computed once per forward pass, reducing matrix recomputation overhead from $O(T \cdot |E| \cdot d^2)$ to $O(|E| \cdot d^2)$.

At each propagation step $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$ ($T=4$, hidden dimension $d=128$):
\begin{align}
m_v^{(t)} &= \sum_{w \in \mathcal{N}(v)} A(e_{vw}) h_w^{(t-1)}, \\
h_v^{(t)} &= \mathrm{GRU}\left(h_v^{(t-1)}, m_v^{(t)}\right).
\end{align}
Global molecular graph aggregation uses multi-head attention pooling:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_v = \frac{\exp(w_\alpha^\top h_v^{(T)})}{\sum_{u \in V} \exp(w_\alpha^\top h_u^{(T)})}, \quad g = \sum_{v \in V} \alpha_v h_v^{(T)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multimodal Fusion}
To combine localized topological graph invariants with global circular substructures and macroscopic physicochemical properties, the graph representation $g \in \mathbb{R}^{128}$ is concatenated with an encoded representation of the flat feature vector $u \in \mathbb{R}^{2066}$ (comprising 2048 Morgan bits, 10 standardized continuous descriptors, and 8 toxicophore alerts):
\begin{equation}
h_{\mathrm{fuse}} = \mathrm{LayerNorm}\left( W_g g + W_u u + b \right),
\end{equation}
followed by a classification head with dropout ($p=0.2$) predicting unnormalized logits $z \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$ for all twelve endpoints simultaneously.

\subsection{Masked Multitask Loss and Positive Balancing}
Let $m_{it} \in \{0, 1\}$ denote the observation mask indicating whether compound $i$ was screened for endpoint $t$, and $y_{it} \in \{0, 1\}$ denote the observed binary label. The masked binary cross-entropy loss is formulated as:
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{\sum_{i,t} m_{it}} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^{12} m_{it} \big[ w_t y_{it} \log(1 + e^{-z_{it}}) \\
+ (1 - y_{it}) \log(1 + e^{z_{it}}) \big],
\end{multline}
where positive weights $w_t = \mathrm{clip}\left(\frac{N_t^-}{N_t^+}, 0.1, 100.0\right)$ compensate for class imbalance.

\subsection{Post-Hoc Calibration and Uncertainty Quantification}
Model confidence scores are calibrated using per-task temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017calibration}. For each endpoint $t$, temperature parameter $T_t > 0$ is optimized by minimizing negative log-likelihood on the held-out validation split:
\begin{equation}
T_t^* = \arg\min_{T_t} \sum_{i \in \mathrm{Val}, m_{it}=1} \left[ \log(1 + e^{z_{it}/T_t}) - y_{it} \frac{z_{it}}{T_t} \right].
\end{equation}
Calibration error is evaluated via Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across $M=10$ equal-frequency confidence bins:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{|B_m|}{N} \left| \mathrm{acc}(B_m) - \mathrm{conf}(B_m) \right|.
\end{equation}
Epistemic uncertainty is estimated at inference time using Monte Carlo Dropout (20 forward passes with stochastic dropout enabled), yielding predictive variance $\sigma_t^2(x)$ for each screening candidate.

\subsection{Self-Supervised Masked Atom Pretraining}
To assess whether unlabeled chemical space can initialize message-passing weights effectively, we pretrain the MPNN encoder using a self-supervised masked-atom prediction objective on 30{,}000 molecules from the ZINC250k library. At each iteration, 15\% of atoms are masked, and the network predicts their elemental species from surrounding context vectors.

Across three epochs of pretraining, the encoder achieves an element prediction accuracy of \textbf{89.91\%} on held-out validation compounds, significantly outperforming the majority class frequency baseline (73.83\% carbon abundance). Transferring these pretrained representations yields rapid convergence during downstream fine-tuning.

\section{System Deployment and Tooling}
The Toxion platform is implemented as a production-grade Python package with full CLI dispatch and interactive tooling:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Automated Chemical Resolver (\texttt{toxion probe}):} Accepts colloquial or IUPAC chemical nomenclature (e.g., ``Bisphenol A'', ``Aspirin''), queries the PubChem REST API with SSL verification and local disk caching, canonicalizes structures, and outputs calibrated multi-endpoint toxicity predictions with Monte Carlo uncertainty intervals.
    \item \textbf{Persistent Database \& Active Retraining:} Integrates a Postgres (Neon) SQLAlchemy database capturing user probes and predictions. When accumulated unobserved compounds reach threshold criteria, semi-supervised pseudo-labeling triggers automated model retraining gated by a validation promotion check.
    \item \textbf{Explainability Engine (\texttt{toxion explain}):} Integrates Integrated Gradients and GNN attention weight visualization, rendering publication-ready atom- and bond-level attribution maps.
    \item \textbf{Interactive Web Visualizer:} A Streamlit web application (\texttt{app/demo\_fusion.py}) supporting real-time molecular structure rendering, property prediction, and epistemic uncertainty inspection.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
In this paper, we presented Toxion, a unified framework for multitask in-vitro toxicity prediction on the Tox21 benchmark. By combining Message Passing Neural Networks with circular Morgan fingerprints, physicochemical descriptors, and leak-free scaffold partitioning, Toxion achieves superior predictive discrimination (0.7753 test ROC-AUC, 0.8153 validation ROC-AUC), particularly on challenging nuclear receptor pathways such as NR-ER (+10.5\% improvement over baseline). Incorporating temperature scaling guarantees calibrated probabilistic outputs (test ECE 0.0332), while external self-supervised pretraining provides effective topological inductive biases. The complete codebase, pretrained weights, and interactive tools are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/ElusiveParadox/toxion}.
